# Relay can own "whose turn is it"

*Competitive landscape for an AI-first kanban board. Research as of 2026-10-11. Feeds the StoryBrand brandscript and the landing page.*

Relay's open ground is the **baton**: a visible, per-card answer to "whose turn is it, human or agent?" across a configurable multi-stage pipeline. As of October 2026 no funded competitor offers that. Everything around it is crowded or taken. Running parallel agents in git worktrees and reviewing diffs is now a free commodity (Conductor, Superset, Cline Kanban, Claude Squad, and more than 100 open-source orchestrators). Phone approvals now ship inside Claude Code itself. "Software factory" belongs to Factory (enterprise, a reported $1.5B+ valuation) and Tessl. "Agents as teammates" belongs to Linear, Jira, Asana and ClickUp. The 2026 consensus pain, "review is the new bottleneck," is widely shared language, and Vibe Kanban said it almost word for word ("The new bottleneck is planning and review") before it shut down in April 2026 because it could not make money from a mostly free tool. Practitioners describe a narrower pain that nobody has turned into a product: they don't know which agent is waiting on them ("Nothing told you"), and they can't step away without the pipeline stalling. Relay's job is to own that problem and to sit between the two loud camps. One camp ("Your life is diffs") reviews every line. The other (the "dark factory") wants no humans at all. Relay's position is a governed middle where humans hold the baton only at decisions, and flow metrics let the factory improve. For the business, Tailwind's 2026 collapse and Vibe Kanban's sunset are the warning. Usage of a free tool does not turn into revenue. The paid offer has to be a deliberate teaching ladder that reaches the developer inside the tool, and it has to end with a "bring it to work" team offer.

## The market split into five camps, and only one is unclaimed

**Software and dark factories.** "Dark factory" comes from Dan Shapiro's "Five Levels" post (Jan 23, 2026). Level 5 is "a black box that turns specs into software… a place where humans are neither needed nor welcome" ([Shapiro](https://www.danshapiro.com/blog/2026/01/the-five-levels-from-spicy-autocomplete-to-the-software-factory/)). StrongDM's Software Factory made the idea concrete, and Simon Willison's write-up spread it. Its rules are "Code **must not be** written by humans" and "Code **must not be** reviewed by humans," and it suggests **$1,000 of tokens per engineer per day** as a benchmark ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/7/software-factory/)). Since then the term has become a commercial category. Factory rebranded around it ("Build Your Software Factory"). Its Factory 2.0 essay (June 15, 2026) says humans "will be responsible for building the factories that build the software" ([Factory 2.0](https://factory.com/news/software-factory)). Its named customers include NVIDIA, EY and Adobe, and it reports a $150M Series C at **$1.5B** (Apr 2026) ([tech-insider](https://tech-insider.org/factory-ai-150-million-series-c-khosla-coding-droids-2026/)). *Unverified and conflicting:* later reports put Factory at $4B (July 2026, Forge Global data) or $5B (Sep 2026), and no primary source confirms either ([Continuum](https://continuumcode.ai/guides/factory-ai-funding/), [CODERCOPS](https://blog.codercops.com/blog/factory-ai-200-million-funding-2026)). Tessl claims "Build your software factory, one skill at a time" ([tessl.io](https://tessl.io/)). Warp has pivoted to "Open infrastructure for cloud software factories" with "factories as code" and observer agents that "open PRs against your factory config to improve it" ([warp.dev](https://www.warp.dev)). That is the closest any vendor comes to Relay's kaizen-of-the-factory idea, but Warp is cloud-hosted, priced per run, and aimed at enterprise. Cognition (Devin plus Windsurf) never says "factory." It sells "the first autonomous software engineer" to Goldman Sachs and Mercedes-Benz ([cognition.com](https://cognition.com/)). It reports a Series D of more than $1B at **$26B** with about $492M ARR (May 2026) ([ChatForest](https://chatforest.com/reviews/cognition-ai-devin-1-billion-round-26b-valuation-492m-arr-2026/)). *Unverified:* a $48B valuation with about $900M ARR (Sep 2026) appears only in secondary blogs ([ValueAddVC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/cognition-valuation-2026-48b-2b-round-900m-arr)).

**Parallel-agent managers.** These are local desktop apps that run N agents in N worktrees, after which you review and merge. Conductor ("Run parallel coding agents on your Mac") raised a **$22M Series A** in March 2026 ([Conductor blog](https://www.conductor.build/blog)). Secondary sources put total funding near $24.5M, and one claim of "$60M across 4 rounds" conflicts and looks unreliable ([StartupIntros](https://startupintros.com/orgs/conductor)). Superset ("Bring Any Agent. Orchestrate Them All.") has **15.1k stars**, a $15/user/month Pro tier, and a status view of agents that are "working, blocked, or waiting on you" ([superset.sh](https://www.superset.sh)). Nimbalyst is the closest analog to Relay's form factor. It puts sessions on a kanban board ("backlog, planning, implementing, complete") and has an iOS app, but its phases are fixed session states with no human/AI stage ownership ([nimbalyst.com](https://nimbalyst.com)). Big vendors now ship the same pattern for free or bundled. OpenAI's Codex app (Feb 2026) is a multi-agent "command center" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-the-codex-app/)). Cursor offers to "launch fleets of agents" ([cursor.com](https://cursor.com)). Claude Code includes Remote Control: scan a QR code, then approve prompts and get push notifications on your phone ([Noqta](https://noqta.tn/en/news/anthropic-claude-code-remote-control-mobile-2026)).

**AI kanban boards.** Vibe Kanban defined this category, with **28.3k stars** and a claimed "over 30,000 active users" ([vibekanban.com](https://www.vibekanban.com)). It is now "sunsetting" and community-maintained ([GitHub](https://github.com/BloopAI/vibe-kanban)). Cline Kanban (Mar 2026) is free and agent-agnostic, and chains dependent cards so they start automatically. Its Review column mixes tasks "blocked waiting for user approval" with finished work ([Cline](https://cline.ghost.io/announcing-kanban/), [azukiazusa](https://azukiazusa.dev/en/blog/cline-kanban/)). Backlog.md (7.0k stars) recommends three human checkpoints (spec, plan, code), but they are agent instructions, not board stages ([GitHub](https://github.com/MrLesk/Backlog.md)). Task Master (28.2k stars) is a task planner, not a board or an executor ([GitHub](https://github.com/eyaltoledano/claude-task-master)). The long tail edges toward Relay's concept but has no visible traction. kandev is a "Kanban workbench assigning a different agent per step behind human gates," Fusion has "plan-review gates," and agent-kanban assigns a different agent to each fixed stage from triage to merge ([awesome-agent-orchestrators](https://github.com/andyrewlee/awesome-agent-orchestrators), [agent-kanban](https://github.com/DylanJones/agent-kanban)). The incumbent trackers have all added "assign to an agent." Linear says "Artificial colleagues. Natural collaboration." and keeps the human as primary assignee with the agent as delegate ([linear.app/agents](https://linear.app/agents)). Jira says "Any agent. Any model. One team." ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira/dev)). ClickUp calls its agents "The World's First Human-like AI Agents" ([ClickUp](https://clickup.com/blog/super-agents-launch/)). Each of them hands work back as a draft PR, so the review gate lives in GitHub, not on the board.

**Human-in-the-loop tooling.** HumanLayer began as an API for agents to request human approval over Slack and email. It has pivoted to "The Multiplayer Coding Agent Workspace," which it describes as "the multiplayer control plane for your software factory." Its six-phase workflow runs "Questions, Research, Design, Structure, Plan, Implement," with the pitch that "Every phase is a place to push back." Pricing runs from free (3 members) to **$100/user/month** ([humanlayer.dev](https://www.humanlayer.dev)). It is the most direct funded competitor to Relay's gated pipeline. Elsewhere, approval is being folded into platforms. Jules gates on plan, then diff, then PR ([jules.google](https://jules.google)). Factory lists "human approval checkpoints" ([factory.com](https://factory.com/)). Omnara pivoted away from mobile control of Claude Code to become a "Managed Agents" alternative with Allow/Deny on risky tool calls ([omnara.com](https://www.omnara.com)). The trend is away from approving each tool call ("babysitting") and toward approving each phase.

**Methodology frameworks.** These are free skill packs, with no board and no hand-off surface. GitHub Spec Kit has roughly 111k–115k stars (secondary), and critics complain of "a sea of markdown documents" ([codemyspec](https://codemyspec.com/blog/best-spec-driven-development-tools)). obra/superpowers is the brainstorm/plan/TDD/debug lineage that Relay's own pipeline borrows from. Its star count is reported at 243k–284k (third-party snapshots, Sep 2026) and **about 292k at v6.4.2** in another guide, so treat the exact figure as unverified ([shyft.ai](https://shyft.ai/repos/obra-superpowers), [developersdigest](https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/claude-code-superpowers-plugin-guide)). Kiro packages the spec-driven method as a paid IDE, priced from $20 to about $200/month ([AI Agent Index](https://theaiagentindex.com/agents/kiro)). These products complement Relay more than they compete with it. Relay's flows are where a method like this actually runs, with gates and visibility.

The empty cell is the combination in Relay's row. No one else pairs a board with human-or-AI ownership of each stage, a configurable graph of agent steps inside a stage (spec → plan → TDD → review → fix → smoke → ship), on-board gates, a mobile surface that shows only what needs you, and flow metrics. The researchers found **no competitor offering flow metrics or value-stream maps** for mixed human-agent work. The nearest partial analogs are Linear's ability to filter Insights by agent delegate ([Linear docs](https://linear.app/docs/agents-in-linear)) and Cline's dependency-aware board ([Cline](https://cline.ghost.io/announcing-kanban/)). This is an absence of evidence from a bounded search, not proof that none exists.

## Customers name attention, not code, as the scarce resource

The 2026 discussion keeps returning to one diagnosis: generating code is cheap, and the human has become the constraint. Matthew Hawthorne, running four Claude Code sessions, wrote that "unless I keep sitting here, actively prompting and reviewing, no work gets done" ([Push to Prod](https://pushtoprod.substack.com/p/stop-babysitting-your-ai-coding-agents)). Bargan Constantin described the failure Relay's "needs you" lane is built for. When an agent stalls, "Nothing told you… You find the stuck one by clicking through them," and the stalled one "is usually the one that was closest to finished." His conclusion: "The scarce resource is not tokens. It is your attention" ([DEV](https://dev.to/bargan/which-claude-code-session-is-waiting-on-you-a-queue-for-agents-that-stopped-to-ask-1ok9)). The clearest evidence that this need is real but unbundled is the **cottage industry of DIY phone-approval hacks**: ntfy push approvers, Telegram approve buttons, a $9.99/month lock-screen yes/no app, and guides titled "Stop 'waiting for approval'… (without going full YOLO)" ([claude-remote-approver](https://github.com/yuuichieguchi/claude-remote-approver), [Pushary](https://pushary.com/fr/claude-code-notifications), [happier.dev](https://guides.happier.dev/how-to-fix-waiting-for-approval-in-claude-code-codex-and-opencode)).

Review is the second pain, and it is consensus language. Shapiro's Level 3 is "Your life is diffs. For many people, this feels like things got worse… almost everyone tops out here" ([Shapiro](https://www.danshapiro.com/blog/2026/01/the-five-levels-from-spicy-autocomplete-to-the-software-factory/)). Secondary coverage of a Harvard study reports **review times up 49%** and agents that "write 30% more code and resolve zero additional issues." That claim was not checked at the primary source ([Forkast](https://forkast.news/ai-coding-agents-write-30-more-code-and-resolve-zero-additional-issues-harvard-study-finds/)). The emotional cost is heavy. On the Bloomberg "productivity panic" thread, developers wrote "My brain feels like a fractured mess," "constantly 3 steps behind," and "a thin, jittery, frayed sort of weariness" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47467922)). Trust lags far behind usage. In Stack Overflow's 2025 survey, **66%** cite "AI solutions that are almost right, but not quite" and only 3% "highly trust" AI output ([InfoWorld](https://infoworld.com/article/4031673/ai-use-among-software-developers-grows-but-trust-remains-an-issue-stack-overflow-survey.html)). The METR randomized trial found experienced developers **19% slower** while they believed they were 20% faster, though it used 2025-era tools ([DX](https://newsletter.getdx.com/p/metr-study-on-how-ai-affects-developer-productivity)).

Developers want two things that pull against each other. They want autonomy: "define 20 tasks, fire them off in the background, go to sleep" ([Push to Prod](https://pushtoprod.substack.com/p/stop-babysitting-your-ai-coding-agents)), or Shapiro's Level 4, "you leave for 12 hours, and check to see if the tests pass." They also want a leash. **60%** block agents from unapproved system changes, and **68%** prefer predictable single-agent setups ([Stack Overflow, "Agents on a leash"](https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/05/27/agents-on-a-leash-agentic-ai-remains-mostly-monitored-at-work)). A board where agents run unattended between explicit gates, and those gates can be answered from a phone, gives them both. Practitioners in the flow and Lean tradition already prescribe this by hand. Yuval Yeret argues for WIP limits as "the valve" in front of reviewers and recommends watching cumulative flow diagrams for a widening review band ([Yeret](https://yuvalyeret.com/blog/ai-coding-made-code-review-the-bottleneck/)). Theory-of-constraints writers note that "optimizing a non-bottleneck step (code generation) does not improve system throughput" ([Georgian AI Lab](https://georgianailab.substack.com/p/review-is-the-new-bottleneck-how)). DORA 2025 calls AI "an amplifier" of whatever system it lands in ([InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/09/dora-state-of-ai-in-dev-2025)). Relay's flow metrics and value-stream maps productize this practitioner work, and nobody else does.

Several territories are open. **"Whose turn is it"** is the clearest. Linear's assignee-versus-delegate split models accountability, not a baton that passes back and forth, and Cline's Review column mixes blocked work with finished work. Nobody makes the current holder a first-class card property across a flow ([Linear docs](https://linear.app/docs/agents-in-linear), [azukiazusa](https://azukiazusa.dev/en/blog/cline-kanban/)). Superset's "waiting on you" status is the nearest phrase, but it is a status indicator, not a positioning. Second, **"only what needs you"** frames mobile and notifications around the human's attention ("it's your turn on 3 cards") rather than control from a phone. That separates Relay from Remote Control and the many approve-button apps. Third, **the governed middle** (Level 4) between "Your life is diffs" and the dark factory. No vendor presents itself as a way to climb Shapiro's levels on purpose. The debate is still framed as a binary between reviewing every diff and running lights-out ([Shapiro](https://www.danshapiro.com/blog/2026/01/the-five-levels-from-spicy-autocomplete-to-the-software-factory/), [Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/7/software-factory/)). Fourth, **kaizen of the factory**: flow metrics, value-stream maps, and WIP limits in front of the review gate. Practitioners like Yeret prescribe this. Warp's observer agents touch it for enterprise clouds. No board offers it. Fifth, **"the board is the factory floor"**, meaning a factory you can *see*, run locally on your own Claude Code. This contrasts with Factory's enterprise SaaS and StrongDM's $1k-a-day black box. Finally, **"bring your method"**: Relay is where Spec Kit or superpowers-style skills actually run, with gates. That is a complement to very popular free frameworks rather than a rival to them ([HumanLayer workflow](https://www.humanlayer.dev), [superpowers](https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/claude-code-superpowers-plugin-guide)).

Three caveats temper this. HumanLayer is funded, gated by phase, and uses factory language, so it could claim the baton quickly. The long tail (kandev, Fusion, Archon) shows the idea occurs to others. And the "baton" concept's appeal has not been tested with users. It is open ground, not proven ground.

## Free tools earn money from the adjacent scarce thing, not from usage

Vibe Kanban is the closest cautionary tale. It had 28.3k stars, a claimed 30,000 active users and 100,000 PRs created. Founder Louis Knight-Webb announced the sunset on **April 10, 2026**. Secondary accounts say Bloop "couldn't monetize a mostly-free tool," and hosted services ended after 30 days ([vibekanban.com](https://www.vibekanban.com), [first-tree.ai](https://first-tree.ai/compare/vs-vibe-kanban)). No primary Bloop post was found, so the date and the 30-day window are secondary. HN users noticed the decline before the announcement: it "stopped improving and still has bugs" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47145436)). The same pattern of shutdowns and pivots runs through the category. Opcode was abandoned, Omnara pivoted, Height shut down in Sept 2025, and HumanLayer's original OSS SDK is "largely deprecated" ([awesome-agent-orchestrators](https://github.com/andyrewlee/awesome-agent-orchestrators), [Creativerly](https://www.creativerly.com/height-app-is-shutting-down/)). For Relay, this means durability and local-first ownership are selling points in themselves, and roughly 30k orphaned Vibe Kanban users are an addressable audience right now.

Tailwind is the structural warning. On Jan 6, 2026, Tailwind Labs **laid off 3 of its 4 engineers** after revenue fell about **80%**, while usage was "growing faster than it ever has." Docs traffic, the funnel to the paid Tailwind Plus, had fallen about 40% because AI tools answer questions without a site visit. Adam Wathan wrote that "if nothing changed, then in about six months we would no longer be able to meet payroll obligations." Sponsors stepped in two days later ([devclass](https://www.devclass.com/2026/01/08/tailwind-labs-lays-off-75-percent-of-its-engineers-thanks-to-brutal-impact-of-ai/), [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/tech-giants-rush-to-sponsor-tailwind-css-after-devastating-layoffs/)). Relay is even more exposed. Its user's main interface is Claude Code, which reads READMEs and skills on the user's behalf. Any paid offer that lives only on a docs page will go unseen. The transitional call to action has to sit inside the agent-mediated path: runner first-run output, empty board states, onboarding. superpowers shows the same gap from the other direction. It has very large adoption (stars unverified, 243k–292k), yet the only visible monetization is "optional commercial support for enterprises" ([developersdigest](https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/claude-code-superpowers-plugin-guide)). A free method packaged as skills does not turn into revenue by itself.

The teaching market is real but thinly documented. It sells in three bands. Two-to-three-week live cohorts cost **$800–$1,500**: Maven's "Elite AI Assisted Coding" listed at $1,500 ([Maven](https://maven.com/kentro/context-engineering-for-coding?promoCode=ISAAC)), Matt Pocock's AI Hero cohort at $795 (third-party guide) ([fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/claude-code-course-guide/)), and Every's one-day Claude Code workshop at $1,000–$1,500 bundled with a membership, with **about 200 attendees, most non-coders** ([Every](https://every.to/courses/claude-code-101)). Premium professional cohorts cost **about $4,000–$5,000**, such as Hamel Husain and Shreya Shankar's AI Evals. Its price signals conflict across third-party listings ($1,995 earlier, $4,200, and $5,000 discounted to $3,750) ([techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/bootcamp_directory/p/maven-ai-engineering-cohorts/), [aiagentrank](https://aiagentrank.io/learn/course/maven-llm-evaluation-hamel)). The third band is book-plus-workshop sold to leaders, like Gene Kim and Steve Yegge's *Vibe Coding* and their leaders' workshop at an enterprise summit ([IT Revolution](https://itrevolution.com/lv25-vibecodingworkshop)). Almost no one discloses revenue or enrollment. Every's 200 is the only hard number found. Each successful teacher runs on an owned audience (a newsletter or X) and uses a free lightning lesson or email course as the transitional offer ([AI Hero listing](https://www.everydev.ai/tools/aihero), [Maven Lightning Lessons](https://maven.com/p/b9a2df)). The $4k+ band works only when the topic is framed as a professional competency that the buyer can expense. That is the work persona, not the hobbyist.

For hobby-to-work, the proven mechanics come from outside AI coding. Tailscale keeps its personal plan "free forever." A custom email domain triggers an automatic business trial, Standard costs $8/user/month, and a "bring Tailscale to work" program offers tiered rewards ([Tailscale pricing FAQ](https://tailscale.com/kb/1251/pricing-faq), [Tailscale blog](https://tailscale.com/blog/free-plan)). Cursor reached an estimated **$1.2B ARR in 2025**, mostly from individuals at $20–40/month, and enterprise grew "as individual developers push for team-wide adoption" (Sacra estimate) ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/cursor)). Its 2026 figures of $2B–$3B ARR come from aggregators that conflict with each other ([gradually.ai](https://gradually.ai/en/cursor-statistics/)). Obsidian dropped its paid commercial license in Feb 2025 because the terms "were confusing" and kept money coming in through Sync and voluntary licenses ([Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/blog/free-for-work/)). The lesson is to charge for a hosted service or a team outcome, not for the right to use the tool at work. Kanban University offers the scalable form of "make money teaching." It sells a credential ladder and certifies trainers to "build your successful Kanban training business," so the method spreads through licensed resellers ([Kanban University](https://kanban.university/build-your-successful-kanban-training-business/)). That requires a named, codified method. For Relay, the method would be the baton and its gate model, not the software.

Put together, the evidence suggests a ladder. The free, local, open board is the lead generator, together with a free email course or starter flow pack placed inside the runner's first-run experience. A self-paced course is the low-ticket step. A live cohort at about $800–$1,500 is the direct offer for individuals. A team workshop with certified internal champions is the "bring it to work" step. Here the expense-it framing supports $4k+ pricing, and a Tailscale-style signal (a GitHub org repo or a company email domain) can trigger the offer. Hosted coordination features (shared boards, mobile push, metrics) are a second possible paid layer, the role Sync plays for Obsidian. No conversion benchmarks were found for free tool to cohort, or for individual to team, in AI-coding education, so every step of this ladder is a hypothesis to test.

## Conclusion

The landscape changes what Relay should sell. Relay's features (parallel agents, worktrees, phone approvals, spec-driven flows) each exist elsewhere, often for free. What no one sells is the *relationship* between human and agent made visible: whose turn it is, why work is waiting, and how the factory improves over time. That makes the baton a strong headline and the kanban board the proof, not the other way around. The emotional language in the market (fractured, behind, babysitting, lonely, "you're a PM now") points to a guide that gives people back their attention and their craft. It does not point to one that promises more throughput, a message the category's fallen leader used right up to its sunset.

The business implication follows the same logic. Relay's moat and its revenue both live in the method, not the binary. The method is a named, teachable way of running a human-plus-agent factory at Level 4, and it can be certified and carried into a team. The free tool must exist and must be excellent, because it is the proof and the lead generator. But Tailwind and Vibe Kanban show that a much-loved free tool earns nothing unless the paid path is designed in from the start. That path has to be reachable even when an agent, not the human, is the one reading the docs.
