## The name and the picture

**Hamaca** is Spanish for hammock. It's also the original Taíno word that English "hammock"
comes from. The picture is **directing the planes from the hammock**: rest that's fully in command.
You're relaxed, and you're running the whole operation.

Two feelings sit under the name:
- **Calm power.** Everyone else is freaking out about AI and their jobs. You're in the hammock, doing
  20 times the work you did before.
- **Play.** Making things is fun again. But what comes out is real software, not toys and not vibe
  code.

We picked it because "Relay" collides with other relay boards, and the hammock says how the product
*feels*, not just what it does.

## The core, in one breath

Hamaca is a kanban board that's also a window onto your software factory. AI agents work the line.
A card's position tells you its state at a glance, and a light comes on only when a decision is
yours. Because watching is cheap, you keep tuning the line and letting go, until you're just the
owner at the edges, shipping from your phone.

## One-liner

> Most developers running AI agents are stuck at their desk, babysitting terminals and never sure
> which agent is waiting on them. Hamaca turns your agents into a factory you can see at a glance.
> They do the work, and your phone lights up only when a decision is yours. You ship more ideas
> than you have hours, from anywhere.

## 1. A character (the hero)

A developer who builds for the love of it, on nights and weekends, and has more ideas than
hours. They already use Claude Code. Later they bring Hamaca to work.

**What they want:** to turn ideas into shipped software without being chained to the desk.

## 2. Has a problem

- **Villain: the silent stall.** An agent finished an hour ago, or hit a question, and nothing told
  you. Work sits idle until you happen to look.
- **External:** agents are cheap now, but coordinating them isn't. Five terminals and five
  worktrees, and you have to re-read everything to find out where each one is. Every context switch costs
  ten minutes.
- **Internal:** you feel like a babysitter instead of a builder. You can't step away. When you do,
  you feel guilty about the agents sitting idle. And you don't quite trust code you never watched
  being made.
- **Philosophical:** a person should only be needed where a person matters. Your judgment is the
  scarce part, so don't spend it watching progress bars.

## 3. Meets a guide

**Empathy:** "We got tired of pacing between terminals too, asking each one 'are you done yet?'"

**Authority:**
- Hamaca is built by Hamaca. Its own board plans, codes, reviews, tests and ships its features.
- It's grounded in lean manufacturing. Toyota's *jidoka* means machines that stop and call a
  human. Its *andon* is the light on the line that says "I need you."
- It ships with flow metrics (lead time, flow efficiency, rework), so you can see the factory
  improve.

## 4. Who gives them a plan

1. **Connect your repo.** Install Hamaca, and Claude Code wires the board to your project.
2. **Draw your line.** Decide which stages are yours (approve, answer) and which belong to agents
   (plan, build, review, test).
3. **Drop in ideas and answer when the light comes on.** Approve, send it back with a note, or
   answer a question, from your phone.

**Agreement plan (the promises):**
- Nothing ships without a person saying so.
- Agents ask instead of guessing.
- You can always see whose move it is.

## 5. And calls them to action

- **Direct:** "Start your board" (sign in, then install).
- **Transitional:** "Watch Hamaca build itself" (the public board). Later: "Learn to run a software
  factory" (the teaching ladder).

## 6. That helps them avoid failure

- Agents finish at 9pm and wait idle until morning.
- Code merges that no human really looked at.
- Burnout from juggling terminals and context.
- Your best ideas die in the backlog, because building them felt like too much babysitting.

## 7. And ends in success

- Ideas get built about as fast as you can describe them. You almost stop keeping a backlog.
- You run five projects at once, because getting your context back takes one glance at a board.
- You ship from the hammock, the trail, or the school pickup line.
- Every week the factory needs you a little less, because you tuned it, and you can prove it got
  better.

## Transformation (identity)

**From babysitter to the one in the hammock.** You stop watching the work and start directing it,
calmly, from wherever you are.
